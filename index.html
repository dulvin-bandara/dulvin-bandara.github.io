Project 01

Accumulator Cooling CFD — M25 & M26

Monash Motorsport · Senior Aerodynamics Engineer, Head of Cooling subsection · 2026

The M26 accumulator is a 120s1p, 6.38 kWh air-cooled pack. Cooling exists to let the pack run a full endurance duty cycle at a smaller capacity: simulation showed a 4.8 kWh pack could match the endurance performance of a heavier uncooled pack, worth roughly 9.4 kg of cell mass.

My role was building and running the CFD that validates the packaged design against the team's thermal targets — segment-level and whole-accumulator models, mass flow boundary conditions across the duct air gaps, and comparison of the resulting pressure drop against hand-calculated predictions.

<20%CFD vs hand-calc pressure drop agreement
5 mmSelected inter-cell air gap
2.81 K/WCell surface to air, at operating point
0.197 W/KTotal pack cooling, against 0.2 target

Duct width was selected by intersecting system pressure-drop curves with the fan curve across candidate air gaps — narrower gaps raise thermal performance but push the operating point past what the fan can deliver. The 5 mm gap sits where the fan curve and system curve cross at a usable mass flow.

Scope note: the thermal resistance modelling and cooling-method trade study (cell body vs tab vs PCM) were produced by teammates. My contribution was the CFD workflow, boundary condition setup and validation against those targets.
